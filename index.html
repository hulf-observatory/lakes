Lake Atlas

How water moves through the land around Hyderabad: the lakes of HMDA, the area that drains into each one (its catchment), the path its water takes to the next lake and on to a river, and how much rain fell on each catchment.

How to use it

  • Tap a lake to see its catchment, the lakes that feed it, the way its water leaves, its rain since February 2026, and how much rain its rooftops could collect.
  • Tap anywhere else to see which lake the water from that spot runs into, or which river if no lake is on the way.
  • Under Map: change the base map, show all streams or catchment outlines, show ward, circle or corporation outlines, and turn on the 3D view. On a phone, tilt with two fingers.
  • The address in the browser changes with each lake you open, so you can share it.

How it was made

Catchments. A catchment is the land that drains into one lake. They were traced from a 30 m elevation model (FABDEM) for all of Telangana: lake beds burned into the terrain, pits filled, flats resolved, then flow direction (D8) and flow accumulation computed, and each lake's catchment traced upstream from its outlet.

Drainage network. From each lake's outlet the flow direction is followed downhill, cell by cell, until it reaches another lake or a river. This gives about 31,500 links between lakes and rivers; the paths follow the terrain, not straight lines.

Streams. Cells that collect water from at least 50 others, ordered by the Strahler method, each tagged with the catchment it lies in.

Rain. Daily rain from Telangana's automatic weather stations (TGDPS). A rain day runs from 08:30 to 08:30 IST, the way the stations count it, and is named by the date it starts. Each catchment's rain is the area-weighted average of the stations nearest to its parts (Thiessen polygons). A station without a reading that day counts as no rain. The HMDA figure is the average of the stations. A gauge whose day is over 50 mm while none of its five nearest stations had a fifth of that is treated as faulty and left out.

Rooftop harvest. Rooftop area × rain × 0.8. Rooftop area is the footprint of the buildings in the catchment (Overture Maps, about 2.57 million buildings in HMDA); 0.8 allows for evaporation and other losses. It covers the dates you pick and is not a yearly figure.

Sources

Elevation: FABDEM (Forest And Buildings removed Copernicus DEM), 30 m. Buildings: Overture Maps Foundation. Lakes, rivers and boundaries: TGRAC. Rain: TGDPS, Planning Department, Government of Telangana. Base map: OpenFreeMap, © OpenStreetMap contributors.

History

Built in 2026 and run by Amruth Kiran on his server until November 2026. Since then it is served as plain files from the observatory, so it no longer needs a database. Rain since then is counted per 08:30 rain day; the earlier version took the highest reading in each calendar day, which counted most rain on two days.
